Chapter 14: The Tutor

Master Alden had taught Pello Ashcombe his letters for two years, and taught
them well — the boy's hand was neater than most nine-year-olds', and his sums
were better than his uncle's had been at that age, by Bellamy Thorne's own
recollection. House Ashcombe had hired him on a physician's recommendation and
never once thought to ask where a tutor of his particular quality had studied,
because the work spoke for itself and the household had larger matters to
attend to than a schoolmaster's history.

"Again," Alden said, tapping the slate. "The founding houses, in order."

Pello recited them without much interest until he reached the end of the list
and stopped, frowning. "That's only eight."

"Very good. Why only eight?"

"Because the ninth one isn't allowed to be said."

Alden's expression didn't change at all, which was itself a small, practiced
skill. "Who told you that?"

"Nobody tells you things like that. You just know them. Like knowing not to
touch the stove."

"That's a wise way to know things," Alden said, and moved the lesson on to
sums before the boy could ask anything else, filing the exchange away with the
same careful attention he gave everything in House Ashcombe's nursery — not
because a nine-year-old's curiosity was dangerous in itself, but because
Thessaly had taught him, long before he'd ever set foot in the Keep, that it
was never wise to assume which small things mattered until much later, when it
was usually too late to matter less.
