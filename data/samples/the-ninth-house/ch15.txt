Chapter 15: The Choir Cipher

Vesper found Sorrel this time, rather than the other way around, waiting on
the Chancery steps at closing with two cups of market tea as though nothing
about the last ten days had been unusual at all.

"You didn't go somewhere safer," Vesper said, handing one over.

"You sent a boy to warn me off. I assumed that meant I was close to something."

"You assumed correctly, which is either promising or extremely inconvenient,
depending on which of us you ask." Vesper sat on the step beside her, dropping
the laundress's stoop she'd worn for months without apparent effort. "The
ledger's cipher. You found the tithe-roll formatting."

"You've read it."

"I wrote half of it, a long time ago, under a different hand." Vesper didn't
explain the different hand, and Sorrel had learned by now not to push where a
door had only opened a crack. "It's built on the Choir Cipher — a private
language, older than either of us, that folds numbers inside dates inside
names. I'll teach you the number-folding. Not the rest."

"Why not the rest?"

"Because the rest isn't mine to teach, and because you're not Choir, whatever
use you've made of yourself so far." Vesper stood, brushing off her skirts.
"Learn the folding. It'll get you through the ledger's middle third. The last
third is someone else's to explain, when you're ready for who that someone
is."

She left before Sorrel could ask who. It was becoming, Sorrel thought, a
frustratingly consistent pattern.
