Chapter 9: The Warden Roster

Warden-Captain Orin Drask ran the Salt Quarter watch the way he ran everything:
with a ledger of his own, no less exacting than Sorrel's, tracking every
Warden's rounds down to the quarter-hour. Casimir Lowe took the eastern docks.
Ettie Marsh took the market row. Pryn Voss took whichever stretch Drask didn't
trust to anyone junior, which this month was most of them. Juno Stray, as
always, took the Bone Market's edge, where the Undercroft's hidden door sat
unremarked behind a fishmonger's stall.

"You're quiet lately," Drask told her, handing over the night's assignments.

"Quiet's the job, Captain."

He let it go, the way he let most things about Juno Stray go — she was his best
Warden and his least explainable one, and over a year of service he had learned
not to ask questions whose answers might cost him a good officer. Whatever else
was unexplainable about her, her Warden's Discipline was never in question: no
one on the roster held a line better under a captain's sharp word.

Across the Quarter, at the Bone Market's edge, a different kind of quiet was
being enforced. Hask — House Vell's huntsman, more comfortable in back alleys
than in Vell Hall's gilded rooms — leaned over a nervous trader's stall and
explained, without raising his voice, that certain goods moving through the
market lately belonged to House Vell's interests whether the trader had asked
permission or not.

"I don't want trouble," the trader said.

"Then you've already agreed with me," Hask said, and left the man's coin purse
lighter and his opinion of House Vell considerably darker, which was, from
Corwin Vell's perspective a street away, exactly the intended outcome.
