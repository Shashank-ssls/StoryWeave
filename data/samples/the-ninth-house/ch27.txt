Chapter 27: A Private Pain

Cassian Ashcombe had told no one, not even Bellamy Thorne, about the headaches
that had begun sometime after the funeral and steadily worsened since — a
pressure behind the eyes that came with flashes of half-formed memory that
weren't his, a stranger's face he half-recognized in his own reflection some
mornings, gone before he could look at it properly.

Tonight was worse than usual. He gripped the edge of his writing desk through
a wave of it, breathing carefully, waiting for the room to stop tilting at the
edges, and in the worst of it heard, with perfect clarity, a name that was not
his own spoken in a voice that was not anyone's he could place: *Kaelen.*

The wave passed, as it always eventually did. Cassian straightened, poured
himself a measure of wine he didn't drink, and sat for a long while in the
dark rather than call for Thorne or Ysolde Fenn, both of whom would have come
at once and both of whom he found himself, for reasons he could not have
explained aloud, reluctant to let see him like this.

He thought of Ysolde's quiet diagnosis at the Vell Masque, overheard rather
than told to him directly — *soul-sight migraine, third one this season* — and
wondered, not for the first time, whether his own private pain belonged to
the same unnamed category as that masked guest's had, and what, exactly, that
category was a symptom of.

He did not have an answer. He had, increasingly, only the name — Kaelen —
surfacing more often now, less like a stranger's and more, unsettlingly, like
a memory trying to remember itself back into being its owner's.
