Chapter 6: The Archivist

Beneath the Bone Market, in a room lined with books no Chancery shelf would ever
hold, an old woman catalogued the Undercroft's collection the way she had for
longer than anyone living could confirm. She went by Thessaly, when she went by
anything at all, and she kept the Umbral Choir's true records in a hand so small
and even that Vesper always said it looked more like lace than writing.

"The scribe followed you," Thessaly said, not looking up from her ledger, when
Vesper came in still smelling of the alley.

"The scribe is more trouble than her wages suggest."

"Most useful people are." Thessaly turned a page. "Watch her. Don't touch her.
Not yet."

Above them, in a wing of Aldenreach Keep far removed from ledgers and archives,
Bellamy Thorne set out the Regent's correspondence in the order Cassian
Ashcombe preferred it — urgent matters first, flattery last, always. Thorne had
served the Ashcombe household since before Cassian could walk unassisted, and
if he had opinions about the Regency, the succession, or the whispers that had
followed House Ashcombe since the funeral, he kept them exactly as private as a
good steward's opinions ought to be kept.

"You're troubled," Cassian said, without looking up from a letter.

"Only tired, my lord."

It was, on that particular evening, entirely true. Thorne would remember,
later, how easy the lie had felt while it was still the honest answer.
