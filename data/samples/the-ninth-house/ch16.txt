Chapter 16: The Name Beneath the Market

The number-folding took Sorrel three nights to learn and one more to apply,
and on the fifth night Vesper finally led her through the fishmonger's stall
and down into the Undercroft itself — a long room of old books that smelled of
dust and, faintly, of the sea seeping through stone from above.

An old woman sat at the far desk, unhurried, as though she had been expecting
this visit for considerably longer than Sorrel had known it was coming.

"You'll be wanting the last third of your cipher," the old woman said, before
Sorrel had said anything at all.

"Vesper said that wasn't hers to explain."

"It isn't. It's mine." The old archivist folded her hands and said, without
any weight at all, that she was the Choirmaster, and had been since before
Sorrel was born. "Thessaly, if names matter to you. They matter to most people
at first. It fades."

Sorrel had built, over the past weeks, a mental picture of the Choirmaster —
something grander, more frightening, than a small woman with ink-stained
fingers surrounded by books. She said as much, which made Thessaly's mouth
twitch toward something almost like amusement.

"Every frightening thing looks smaller once you've sat across a desk from
it," Thessaly said. "The ledger you're carrying is a Choir accounting book,
Sorrel Quill — three centuries of names we kept when keeping them was the only
thing standing between the Ninth House and being erased entirely. Your last
third isn't numbers. It's names. And one of them, I think, is going to matter
to you more than you're prepared for."

She did not say which name, not yet. But she slid the ledger's final page
toward Sorrel across the desk, and waited.
