Chapter 40: The Choosing

The Ashen Court filled at dawn and did not empty until dusk, which Magistrate
Wyle later called, without irony, the most thoroughly deliberated Choosing of
his long career on the circuit. Meraude Vell spoke for House Vell with every
bit of the composure that had built her house's standing, and did not, in the
end, raise the question she and Corwin alone understood the weight of. Fennick
and Brenna Oswald cast House Oswald's voice early and, as ever, usefully.
Ione Ashcombe stood at her brother's side through the whole long day and did
not once let her composure slip, whatever private things she had come, by
then, to suspect about him.

Cassian Ashcombe was named Thornmere's monarch as the light went gold over the
Ashen Court's old stones, Magistrate Wyle's certification read aloud in a
voice that carried to every corner of the crowd, and if the newly-named king's
own relief, in that moment, ran deeper and stranger than any ordinary
successor's might have, only a very small circle of Aldenreach understood
exactly why.

Sorrel Quill watched from the Chancery scribes' gallery, where junior clerks
had always watched such things, her name still just Sorrel Quill in every
public record Thornmere kept — no seal, no seat, no line in any genealogy the
crown would ever officially recognize. She found, watching the crowd's roar
rise around a king who was and was not entirely who they believed him to be,
that she minded this considerably less than she once might have guessed she
would.

Beside her, unnoticed by the gallery's other clerks, Vesper leaned close.

"Well, chancery girl. Was it worth it?"

Sorrel watched the Ashen Court settle into evening, the bell that had once
rung for a dead king now ringing, differently, for a living one.

"Ask me again next year," she said. "I suspect the answer will keep changing."
