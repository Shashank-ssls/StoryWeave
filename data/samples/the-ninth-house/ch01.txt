Chapter 1: The Mourning Bell

Thornmere buried its king on a grey morning, and the bell over Aldenreach rang for
so long that people stopped counting the tolls and simply waited for the silence
after. Sorrel Quill counted anyway. It was a scribe's habit; she counted everything.

She had not known King Aldric, not really — no one in the Chancery of Records had,
except by the paper he left behind, and that was Sorrel's whole acquaintance with
most of the realm. Today's task was the same as any other day's: catalogue what the
dead man's household surrendered to the crown's ledgers before it passed to the
Regency. A ring here. A letter there. A box of seals no one could place, stamped
with a device the Chancery's own reference books did not carry.

"Leave the box," said Denna Ashford, not looking up from her own stack. "Master
Vane wants the jewelry catalogued by evening, not whatever that is."

"It might be worth knowing what it is."

"It might be worth your evening, is what it might be worth."

Sorrel catalogued the jewelry first, because Denna was usually right about which
battles were worth having. Quickhand, Denna called it once, half a joke, the way
Sorrel's fingers sorted a stack faster than her eyes seemed to read it — and she
kept the sealed box near her elbow the whole while, and once, sorting through a
courtier's returned letters, she found a single line that made her stop entirely:
a warning, half a century old, never to speak the name of the Ninth House within
the palace walls, for the walls themselves were said to still be listening.

She wrote it down anyway, in the private hand she used for nothing important, and
told herself it meant nothing.

Outside, the Mourning Bell rang once more, and stopped.
