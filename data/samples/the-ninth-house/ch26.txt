Chapter 26: What Corwin Saw

Corwin Vell had made a habit, these past months, of drifting past Sorrel's
desk on his invented errands, and habits — even invented ones — occasionally
pay unexpected dividends. On an evening Sorrel had left her working copy of
the ledger's translated pages unlocked longer than she should have, Corwin
drifted past at exactly the wrong moment for her and exactly the right one for
him, and read enough, upside-down and at a glance, to recognize names he had
no business recognizing from a common tax ledger.

He said nothing to Sorrel. He said nothing, at first, even to his aunt. He
took the observation instead to Hask, whose discretion he trusted more than
most of House Vell's more official channels.

"The chancery girl's ledger isn't about taxes," Corwin said. "Or not only
about taxes. There were names on that page that belong to houses the crown
declared extinct before either of us was born."

Hask, who had spent considerably more time in the Salt Quarter's less
respectable corners than Corwin had, absorbed this with the flat attention of
a man already calculating its use. "You think she's Choir."

"I think she's something House Vell would be foolish not to understand better
before House Ashcombe understands it first." Corwin's ambitions, as Denna
Ashford had once warned Sorrel, ran considerably deeper than his pleasant
manner suggested. "Watch her. Quietly. My aunt doesn't need to know yet — not
until there's something worth telling her."

Hask nodded once, and from that evening forward, Sorrel Quill acquired a
shadow she never once noticed following her.
