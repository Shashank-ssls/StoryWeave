Chapter 37: The Grey Road's Threshold

Mira had not been herself since the Undercroft collapse — quieter, prone to
staring at nothing for long stretches, humming the wordless tune Thessaly had
once called older than the Choir with a new, unsettled intensity. Sorrel found
her one evening sitting alone near the rebuilt archive's threshold, the
doorway the Choir had taken to calling the Grey Road for reasons no one had
fully explained to her yet.

"You don't have to sit out here alone," Sorrel said, settling beside her.

"I like the threshold." Mira didn't look up. "It's the only place in the
Undercroft that feels like it's between things instead of just one thing.
I've always liked between-places. I don't know why."

"Most children like doorways. It's not so strange."

"I'm not sure I'm the age I look, is the trouble." Mira finally turned, and
something in her young face, just then, seemed older than eleven years had
any right to seem. "I know the names of places I've never been, Sorrel. Not
guessed. Known, the way you know your own street. I always thought everyone
carried names like that and simply didn't mention it."

Sorrel sat very still, recognizing the particular shape of a confession
before it had fully arrived.

Mira Quell had always known the names of places she had never been, and now,
on the Grey Road's threshold, she finally said why: she had walked in from
outside Thornmere altogether, a Wanderer poured into a Choir initiate's small
body.

"I don't remember choosing this body," Mira said quietly. "I just remember
arriving in it, very young, and knowing things a child that young shouldn't
know. I never told Thessaly. I don't know why I'm telling you first."

"Maybe," Sorrel said, taking her small hand, "because I finally know what it's
like to carry a name that isn't only your own."
