Chapter 25: What Should Have Happened

"It should have warmed," Thessaly said, taking the Marrow Seal back and
turning it over in her own hands as though it might explain itself to her
where it hadn't to Sorrel. "Every Marrow who's held this in three centuries —
and there have been perhaps a dozen, no more — has felt it warm to their
blood within moments. That it did nothing at all troubles me considerably
more than if it had scorched your palm."

"Meaning what? That I'm not really a Marrow after all?"

"Meaning I don't yet know what it means, and I dislike saying that as much as
you dislike hearing it." Thessaly set the seal back in its case, more gently
than the moment's frustration seemed to warrant. "There is a name in the old
records I have avoided saying aloud to you until I understood more. I no
longer think waiting will make me understand more. Kaelen the Unmaker was the
last sorcerer-king the Ninth House produced before the Long Silence took it —
the most powerful practitioner of Marrow-craft Marrowfall ever raised, named
for the Unmaking itself, the technique that could unmake a soul from its body
as easily as freeing a coin from a purse, and the reason the crown grew
frightened enough to move against us at all. His own bloodline was believed to
have ended with him, exactly as ours was believed to have ended with the
fall."

"You think he's connected to this somehow. To the seal not answering."

"I think," Thessaly said carefully, "that the seal answers to a soul it
recognizes, not merely a bloodline it doesn't. And I think that possibility
frightens me considerably more than a stubborn piece of silver ever could."

Vesper, watching from the doorway, said nothing at all — but her silence, this
time, was its own kind of confirmation.
