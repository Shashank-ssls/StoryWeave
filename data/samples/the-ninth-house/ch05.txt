Chapter 5: The Grey Sash

Sorrel told herself she was only walking Vey home to ask a second question about
the Ninth House, phrased more carefully than the first. It was a poor lie even by
her own standards, and she knew it, and went anyway.

Vey did not go home. She went to the Undercroft — a low door behind the Bone
Market that Sorrel had walked past a hundred times without once wondering where
it led — and standing in the alley's last grey light, Mistress Vey untied her
apron, and under it she wore the grey sash of the Choir — Vesper had never been
a laundress at all.

"You were meant to be three streets behind me by now," Vesper said, without
turning around. "You're worse at this than you think you are."

Sorrel's first instinct was to run. Her second, stronger one, was to ask
questions, which was the instinct that had gotten her into this in the first
place and the one she trusted more.

"The Choir," Sorrel said. "You're Umbral Choir."

"I'm a woman who does other people's laundry and occasionally other people's
listening," Vesper said. "The name is only useful to people who already know
what it means. You don't, yet. Go home, Sorrel. This once, I'm asking instead
of telling."

"And if I don't?"

Vesper finally turned, and for a moment her ordinary, forgettable face looked
neither ordinary nor forgettable at all.

"Then Thursday's cuffs will be the least complicated thing in your week."

Sorrel went home. But she did not stop asking.
