Chapter 8: What Sorrel Kept

Ser Robart Kell came to the Chancery himself, which was unusual enough that
Denna Ashford raised an eyebrow at Sorrel across the room before he had even
finished crossing it.

"Master Vane says you've a ledger that's given you trouble," Kell said. "The
Regent's household will take it off your hands, if it's proving difficult."

It would have been so easy to hand it over. Sorrel had rehearsed handing it
over, on the walk in that morning, as the obviously correct thing to do — a
water-stained ledger, a guard-captain's polite request, nothing worth a
chancery girl's stubbornness.

"It's not difficult," she heard herself say instead. "Just slow. I'll have it
finished within the week."

Kell studied her a moment longer than the answer required, nodded, and left.
Denna didn't say anything until he was well out of earshot.

"That was a lie."

"It was an optimistic estimate."

"Sorrel."

"I want to know what it says before someone decides I shouldn't."

In the Council chamber that same afternoon, Meraude Vell made House Vell's
ambitions plainer than she had all week: a marriage between the Houses, she
proposed — Tobin Vell to Ione Ashcombe, binding the realm's two strongest lines
before the Choosing could set them against each other. Cassian did not refuse
outright. He simply did not answer, which Meraude, watching him as closely as
anyone in Thornmere, understood was its own kind of answer, and filed away for
later use.
