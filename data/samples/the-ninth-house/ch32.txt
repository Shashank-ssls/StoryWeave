Chapter 32: An Empty Drawer

Corwin Vell's patience, never his strongest quality, finally gave out after
weeks of quiet watching produced nothing more concrete than a name half-seen
upside-down on a page. He gave Hask a simple instruction: find the ledger
itself, whatever it truly was, and bring back proof House Vell could use.

Hask broke into the Chancery of Records two hours past midnight, moving
through the dark building with the practiced quiet of a man who had done this
sort of work far more often than his employer's household suspected. Sorrel's
desk was easy enough to find. Her locked drawer took him under a minute to
open.

It was empty, save for tax rolls and a half-finished letter to no one in
particular.

Sorrel had moved the ledger to the Undercroft a full week earlier, on
Thessaly's quiet advice, after Vesper reported — carefully, without naming a
source — that someone had been asking questions about a chancery scribe's
comings and goings. The timing had felt, at the time, like ordinary caution.
It would prove, within hours, to have been considerably more than that.

Hask left the Chancery as quietly as he'd entered it, empty-handed and
irritated, and reported back to Corwin only that the girl was more careful
than either of them had credited her for — which Corwin took, correctly, as
confirmation that there had been something worth finding in the first place,
and simply relocated somewhere he would now have to work harder to reach.
