Chapter 30: What Thorne Chose Not to Say

Cassian returned to his duties within the week, publicly attributing the
collapse to overwork, and the Council accepted the explanation because it was
convenient and because no one wished to imagine a worse one. Bellamy Thorne
accepted nothing so easily. He had arranged the Regent's correspondence for
twenty years and knew the particular rhythms of the man's handwriting, his
pauses, the way he held a pen when a letter troubled him — and all of it had
changed, subtly, since that night. Not worse. Simply different, the way a
familiar room looks different after the furniture has been quietly rearranged
in the dark.

He said nothing of it to Ysolde Fenn, who he suspected knew more than she'd
let on. He said nothing to Ione, who would have asked questions he had no
answers for. He certainly said nothing to Ser Robart Kell, who dealt in
threats and could be trusted to make a threat of anything unexplained.

"You're troubled," Cassian said one evening, echoing, without either of them
noticing the echo, the exact words he had once used to ask the same question
of Thorne himself.

"Only tired, my lord," Thorne said again, and this time the lie cost him
considerably more than it had the first.

He kept the Regent's secrets the way he had always kept them — completely,
without complaint, and without once asking to be told more than he'd been
given. It was, he told himself, simply loyalty doing what loyalty had always
done. He did not examine, too closely, the fear sitting quietly beneath it.
