Chapter 10: The Vell Chin

Rain caught Sorrel between the Chancery and home, hard enough that she ducked
under the Bone Market's awnings rather than finish the walk soaked. A Warden
was already sheltering there, hood up against the downpour, and offered a
short nod of the kind strangers give each other in bad weather.

Sorrel had spent that same afternoon in the Chancery's portrait archive,
matching a decade of Vell family commissions to their catalogue numbers for a
tax assessment — tedious work that had, at least, filled her head with faces:
Meraude's sharp brows, Tobin's soft jaw, and a third face from an older
painting, a daughter's, listed in the ledger only as "S. Vell, deceased or
departed, status unconfirmed."

The wind gusted hard enough to catch the Warden's hood and pull it back before
she could catch it herself. Warden Juno Stray pulled back her hood, and Sorrel
saw the Vell chin she'd only known from a portrait: Sable Vell, the Duchess's
vanished daughter, had been standing watch in the Salt Quarter for a year.

Neither of them said anything for a long moment. Rain filled the silence
better than words would have.

"You're the chancery girl," Juno said finally, pulling the hood back up. "The
one with the ledger everyone's suddenly interested in."

"You're supposed to be dead. Or gone. The record isn't clear which."

"The record's wrong on purpose. I'd like it to stay that way." Juno's voice
was level, not pleading — a request stated once, plainly, by someone unused to
asking for anything twice. "You keep your secrets. I'll keep mine. That seems
fair, chancery girl."

Sorrel walked home in the rain after all, and found she didn't mind it.
