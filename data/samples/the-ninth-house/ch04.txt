Chapter 4: A Cipher Resists

The water-damaged ledger did not yield to Sorrel's usual tricks. She tried it
against the standard Chancery code first, the one clerks used for tax
shorthand, and got nonsense. She tried three older ciphers pulled from the
reference shelves, each stranger than the last, and got more elegant nonsense.

"You're going to ruin your eyes," Denna Ashford said, not unkindly, setting a
second lamp on Sorrel's desk without being asked. Denna's own stack had long
since been finished and returned; she stayed later than she needed to most
nights, which Sorrel had never asked her about and probably should have.

"I keep thinking it isn't a cipher at all," Sorrel said. "I keep thinking it's a
cipher pretending to be water damage."

"That's an expensive kind of thinking for a scribe's wage."

Master Vane's newest favorite, a Vell cousin named Corwin Vell, chose that
evening to drift through the Chancery on some invented errand, the way he did
perhaps twice a week — always polite, always watching who was working late and
on what. He paused at Sorrel's desk just long enough to glance at the ledger's
open page, said something pleasant about the weather, and drifted on toward the
door.

"He does that," Denna said, once he was gone. "Don't mistake it for interest in
you personally. He does it to everyone eventually."

Sorrel wasn't so sure. She closed the ledger, banked the lamps, and walked home
through streets that felt, for the first time since the funeral, like they were
being watched back.
