Chapter 34: Blood Was Only the Door

They salvaged what they could from the Undercroft by lamplight, Sorrel working
alongside Vesper and Mira long past exhaustion, and it was in the middle of
that grim, dusty labor that Thessaly finally said the rest of what she had
been withholding since Marrowfall.

"The seal not answering you," Thessaly said, setting aside a cracked ledger
box, "has troubled me for weeks, and tonight, watching you carry half this
archive's weight without once asking to stop, I finally understood why I'd
been asking the wrong question."

"Which question was that?"

"Whether you were Marrow enough." Thessaly straightened, and in the lamplight
her face looked older and, somehow, gentler than Sorrel had yet seen it. "It
was not blood that had made her a Marrow, Thessaly said — blood was only ever
the door. The soul that opened it had done this before, and Aurelia Marrow
was the name it wore the last time."

Sorrel set down the box she was carrying very carefully, as though the room
itself might shatter if she moved too quickly. "You're saying I'm not her
descendant. You're saying I'm her."

"I'm saying the seal doesn't warm to bloodlines. It warms to a soul it
recognizes returning to a body that can carry it — and it didn't warm for you
because it already knew you, the way an old friend doesn't need reintroducing.
The line only ever mattered as the door blood built for a soul to come back
through. You walked through your own door a long time before you knew there
was one."

Sorrel looked down at her own hands — a scribe's hands, ink-stained,
unremarkable — and found she no longer entirely recognized them.
