Chapter 39: What Sorrel Kept, and What She Gave Away

Thessaly had never once suggested Sorrel ought to claim anything — no seat, no
title, no reversal of three centuries' careful hiding — but Sorrel had spent
enough sleepless nights turning the question over herself that she no longer
needed anyone to ask it for her before she'd found an answer.

"I don't want the Marrow name," she told Thessaly, in the rebuilt archive, the
Marrow Seal sitting between them on the desk where it had sat, largely
untouched, since Marrowfall. "Not as a throne. Not as anything the Council
would need to argue about. Aurelia Marrow is a name I carried once, a very
long time ago, and I think I'm allowed to have finished carrying it."

"No one would blame you for wanting differently."

"I know." Sorrel pushed the seal gently across the desk. "But I think the
Ninth House's hiding has cost more than it's protected, these past three
centuries — a girl who didn't know her own name until she was grown, a
child who walked in from somewhere else entirely and told no one for years
because she didn't know it was safe to. I'd rather spend what this name is
worth on making that safer for whoever comes after, than on a seat I never
wanted in the first place."

Thessaly took the seal back slowly, weighing it, and weighing, too, the
scribe in front of her who had just quietly offered to begin unmaking a habit
of secrecy far older than either of them.

"That's not a small thing you're proposing."

"No," Sorrel agreed. "But I think it's the right one. The Choir doesn't have
to stay hidden forever just because it always has. Let's start small. Let's
start with the truth costing less than it used to."
