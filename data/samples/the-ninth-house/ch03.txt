Chapter 3: The Salt Quarter

The Salt Quarter smelled of tar and low tide, and Sorrel went there for the same
reason half the Chancery's junior scribes did: Mistress Vey's laundry took ink
stains out of good linen for less than anyone in the upper city, and asked no
questions about how they got there.

"Long day," Vey said, not a question, taking Sorrel's cuffs without being asked.
She was an unremarkable woman, grey-haired, quick-handed, with the kind of face
that made you feel you had met her before even the first time.

"Long year," Sorrel said, and meant the ledger, and the box of unplaceable seals
still sitting on her desk, and the word she had written down and not been able to
put out of her mind since.

A Warden passed the shopfront on her rounds — Juno Stray, by the badge, a young
watchwoman with a soldier's economy of movement and eyes that missed very little
of the street. She nodded once at Vey, the way people who saw each other daily
nod, and moved on toward the Bone Market without breaking stride.

"You're wanted for gossip, not questions," Vey said, when Sorrel didn't move to
leave. "But go on. Ask your question."

"Have you ever heard of the Ninth House?"

Vey's hands did not stop working the linen. That was, Sorrel would realize much
later, the most careful non-answer she had ever been given.

"Everyone's heard of the Ninth House," Vey said. "It's a story you tell children
so they don't ask about the old wards on the Undercroft doors. Go home, Sorrel
Quill. Your cuffs will be ready Thursday."
