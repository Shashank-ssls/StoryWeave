Chapter 23: A Quiet Watch

Casimir Lowe and Pryn Voss were covering the Bone Market's night rotation
together when Casimir noticed something he couldn't quite name: a pattern of
foot traffic near the fishmonger's stall, too regular to be customers, too
varied in who walked it to be a single smuggling crew.

"Same door, different people, every few nights," he told Pryn. "Could be
nothing."

"Could be something the raid didn't catch." Pryn had been on the smuggling
raid herself and still felt, faintly, that they'd missed something larger than
what they'd seized. "Worth flagging to the Captain."

Orin Drask listened to their report the next morning with the same careful
attention he gave every report, and then surprised them both by ordering
nothing more dramatic than continued, quiet observation — no second raid, no
questioning of the fishmonger, nothing that might announce the Wardens had
noticed anything at all.

"If it's smuggling, a quiet watch finds us the whole network eventually,"
Drask said. "If it's something else" — he did not elaborate on what else
might mean, and neither Casimir nor Pryn pressed him on it — "then a second
raid just teaches them to be more careful; and I'd rather understand a thing
before I frighten it into hiding better."

It was, Juno Stray thought, overhearing the exchange from across the
guardroom, either the luckiest instinct Orin Drask had ever had, or a far more
dangerous kind of perceptiveness than she'd previously credited him with. She
resolved, either way, to be considerably more careful about which nights she
used that door herself.
