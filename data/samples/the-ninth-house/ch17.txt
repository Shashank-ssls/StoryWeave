Chapter 17: Invitations

House Vell sent out invitations to the Vell Masque a full month in advance,
which everyone in Aldenreach understood as its own small act of political
theater — a month was long enough to be noticed, and short enough that no
rival house could easily arrange one of their own first.

Lord Fennick Oswald read his invitation twice before passing it to his sister.
"They'll use it to press the marriage question again. Tobin and Ione."

"Then we should arrive early and press a question of our own," Brenna Oswald
said. House Oswald was the smallest of the Council's seats, and both siblings
had long ago learned that smallness, used well, could still buy a seat at
larger tables. "If House Vell binds itself to House Ashcombe, House Oswald
wants a binding of its own before the ink dries on theirs. A lesser match is
still a match."

"You're thinking of Corwin."

"I'm thinking of Corwin's ambition, which is considerably larger than his
current station. A cousin who marries well and rises with House Vell's
fortunes is worth cultivating early, not after he's already risen."

Fennick did not entirely approve — Corwin's reputation for watching rooms
rather than joining them unsettled him in ways he found hard to articulate —
but he had learned, over many years of being the smaller house at every
table, not to argue with his sister's instincts for which doors were worth
knocking on before anyone else thought to try the handle.

The invitation itself, when it finally arrived at the Chancery for the record,
crossed Sorrel's desk along with a hundred others, and she filed it without a
second thought, not yet knowing she would be attending in a capacity no
invitation had ever intended for a scribe.
