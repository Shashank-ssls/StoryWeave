Chapter 20: The Last Name

Thessaly did not soften it, when Sorrel came asking, and Sorrel would later be
grateful for that more than for almost anything else the old woman ever told
her.

"You want the rest of the names," Thessaly said. "You've already found one of
them. You simply don't recognize it yet, because it's been hiding under
another one your whole life."

She turned the ledger to its final page and set one ink-stained finger beneath
a line Sorrel had read a dozen times without it meaning anything at all. The
ledger's last page named the scribe who would carry it: not Sorrel Quill, a
chancery orphan, but Aurelia Marrow, the Ninth House's last acknowledged
daughter.

"That's not possible," Sorrel said, and heard how thin her own voice sounded
saying it. "I grew up at the Aldenreach orphan house. Everyone there grew up
at the Aldenreach orphan house. There's nothing — noble — about any of it."

"The Marrow line went to ground three generations ago, and every generation
since has gone to ground a little more carefully than the last, until finally
a Marrow child was left exactly where no one — not the crown, not House Vell,
not even most of the Choir — would think to look for one." Thessaly's voice
was gentle now, gentler than Sorrel had yet heard it. "An orphan house ledger
with a smudged surname. A name close enough to Marrow's old shorthand that no
clerk in three generations thought twice about it. Quill, Sorrel. Think about
what a quill is made from."

Sorrel thought about it. She thought about it for a very long time, sitting
in the Undercroft's low light, before she could make herself say anything at
all.
