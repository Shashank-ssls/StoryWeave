Chapter 13: The Salt Quarter Raid

Orin Drask had been building his case against the Bone Market's smuggling ring
for two months, and when he finally moved, he moved with every Warden he had.
Casimir Lowe and Pryn Voss took the eastern stalls. Ettie Marsh held the market
row itself, sealing the exits. Juno Stray was given the Undercroft's own
street — the one assignment, of every assignment Drask could have handed out
that night, that put her closest to the door she most needed no one else to
notice.

The raid went well, by every measure Drask cared about: three smugglers taken,
a season's worth of untaxed salt seized, no Wardens badly hurt. It went less
well by the measure only Juno was keeping. A frightened smuggler, cornered
near the fishmonger's stall, nearly bolted straight through the Undercroft's
hidden door rather than face arrest — and only Juno's quick hand on his collar,
redirecting him bodily toward Ettie Marsh instead, kept him from finding it.

"Good catch," Ettie said afterward, hauling the man off.

"Lucky catch," Juno said, and meant it more literally than Ettie understood.

Drask found her afterward, going over the night's tally by lamplight. "You did
well tonight."

"Just doing the job, Captain."

He studied her again, the way he sometimes did, weighing the good officer
against the unexplainable one, and — as always — let the good officer win the
argument. It would be, Juno thought, watching him walk away, the last time
that particular arithmetic came out so easily in her favor.
