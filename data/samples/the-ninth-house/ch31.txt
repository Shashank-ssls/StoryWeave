Chapter 31: Two Secrets, One Room

The Chancery's quarterly audit brought Sorrel into the Regent's presence for
the first time since the Council's first sitting, months earlier, when she had
been nothing more than a scribe carrying a ledger too heavy to be interesting
to anyone above her station. She was still, by any official account, exactly
that. She no longer felt like it, walking into the audit chamber and finding
Cassian Ashcombe already seated, reviewing figures with Bellamy Thorne at his
elbow.

"Mistress Quill," Cassian said, with the polite, distant courtesy of a man
greeting someone he did not expect to remember afterward. "Master Vane speaks
well of your work."

"Thank you, my lord." Sorrel kept her eyes on the ledgers between them, aware,
with an unfamiliar prickling clarity, of something in the Regent's bearing
that hadn't been there at the first sitting — a stillness around the eyes, a
carefulness in how he held himself, that she now recognized, having spent
weeks learning to recognize the shape of a secret from the outside.

Cassian, for his part, found himself watching the young scribe with an
attention the audit did not require, unable to name why, only that something
about her unsettled the same quiet place behind his eyes where the headaches
lived, as though two unrelated pressures had briefly, inexplicably, leaned
against the same wall from opposite sides.

Neither said anything beyond the audit's plain business. The figures balanced.
The meeting ended on schedule. And both of them left the chamber carrying
exactly as much unspoken as they had carried into it — perhaps, though neither
yet knew it, rather more.
