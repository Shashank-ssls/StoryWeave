Chapter 7: The Youngest Claims

Mira Quell was eleven years old, quick as any Bone Market pickpocket, and utterly
devoted to Thessaly, who fed her better than the orphan house ever had and asked
only that she carry sealed letters without reading them. Mira read them anyway,
sometimes, and understood perhaps a third of what she read, and told no one that
either.

"You're humming," Thessaly said, without looking up.

"I don't know the words. Just the tune." Mira set the day's letters on the desk.
"Is it a Choir song?"

"It's older than the Choir." Thessaly almost smiled. "Go on. Oswin's waiting on
you at the market stalls."

Elsewhere, in a Council chamber that smelled of old wax, House Ashcombe pressed
its own quiet case: that Pello Ashcombe, Cassian's young nephew, stood closest
in blood to the old line, should the Choosing ever require a child-regent
instead of an adult ruler. Meraude Vell dismissed the notion outright — "the
realm will not be governed from a nursery" — but Corwin Vell, seated near the
back, watched the argument with more interest than his aunt did, as though
weighing which outcome served House Vell best rather than which was true.

Pello himself, nine years old and bored past endurance by the whole sitting,
spent the hour counting the knots in the chamber's oak ceiling beams and came
away from his first Council appearance certain only that adults argued even
more than his tutors did.
