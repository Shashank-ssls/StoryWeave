Chapter 18: The Vell Masque

Thessaly's request had been simple enough to state and considerably harder to
carry out: attend the Vell Masque, masked like every other guest, and watch
who Corwin Vell spoke to when he thought no one worth mentioning was watching.
A chancery scribe could not walk through Vell Hall's front doors, but a masked
guest in a borrowed gown, slipped in through a service entrance Vesper knew
well, could manage it easily enough.

The masque itself was overwhelming in the particular way only the upper city
knew how to be overwhelming — candlelight doubled by mirrors, music too
elaborate to follow, masked faces that could have belonged to anyone. Sorrel
recognized Corwin Vell by his walk before she recognized his mask, and spent
the better part of an hour trailing him at a discreet distance as he moved
between conversations that all seemed, on inspection, to be about the same
thing: who would benefit, and how much, from various arrangements of marriage
and succession.

Near the garden doors, a masked guest stumbled and caught the wall, one hand
pressed hard against her temple. Maester Ysolde Fenn reached her within
moments — House Ashcombe's physician attended every major gathering, quietly,
for exactly this reason — and guided her to a side room with the brisk
efficiency of long practice.

"Soul-sight migraine," Ysolde murmured to no one in particular, once the
guest's mask was carefully removed. "Third one this season." She pressed a
folded cloth to the woman's temple and said nothing further, though her eyes,
for just a moment, found the crowd beyond the door and searched it as though
looking for a cause rather than merely a patient.

Sorrel, watching from the doorway, understood none of what she'd just seen,
and would not understand it for weeks yet.
