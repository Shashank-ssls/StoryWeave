Chapter 33: The Undercroft Collapse

It took Hask only three nights of watching to trace Sorrel's new route through
the Bone Market to the fishmonger's stall, and one night more to work out that
the stall's back wall was not, in fact, a wall at all. He did not tell Corwin
before trying the door himself — a mistake born of the same impatience that
had cost him the empty drawer, and one he would have considerable time to
regret.

The Undercroft's hidden door was warded, in ways the Bone Market's ordinary
locks were not, and a stranger's hand forcing it in the dead of night woke
something in the old stonework that had slept, mostly undisturbed, since long
before Hask's grandfather was born. The ward did not care that Hask himself
meant no particular harm to the books he'd come to find. It cared only that
an unrecognized hand had forced a door it had been set, centuries ago, to
protect.

Stone cracked along an old fault line above the archive's east wall. Oswin,
sleeping in a side alcove, was caught beneath a fall of loosened brick and
pulled clear only by Mira, screaming for help loud enough to bring Vesper
running from two streets away. Thessaly's ledgers — three centuries of names,
painstakingly kept — survived largely intact, buried under rubble rather than
burned, which Thessaly would later call the one mercy in an otherwise ruinous
night.

Hask fled before the dust had settled, badly shaken and entirely without the
proof Corwin had sent him for. The Undercroft's location, kept secret for
longer than anyone living could remember, was compromised in a single
careless hour — and everyone who called it home understood, standing amid the
wreckage by lamplight, that nothing about their careful, quiet existence would
be quite so quiet again.
