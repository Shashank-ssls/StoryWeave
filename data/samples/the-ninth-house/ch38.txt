Chapter 38: What Meraude Chose

Cassian had known what he carried for weeks now — Thessaly had told him
plainly, once he'd woken from the collapse — and had decided, quietly and
alone, that a name three centuries buried did not disqualify a man from the
work the realm still needed done. He would stand for the Choosing.

Corwin finally brought his aunt the full weight of what he and Hask had
gathered — the ledger, the Undercroft, the chancery girl's true name, and,
because Hask's ruined night at the Undercroft had at least bought that much,
a strong suspicion of what ailed the Regent himself. Meraude Vell listened to
all of it without interrupting, and sat with it a long while after Corwin had
finished.

"You could end House Ashcombe's Choosing before it begins," Corwin said. "A
Regent carrying a Ninth House soul — the Council would never confirm him.
House Vell would stand unopposed."

"And House Vell would govern a realm that had just learned its own
Regent-elect was, by rumor if nothing provable, an ancient sorcerer's
returned soul." Meraude's voice was flat, calculating in the way that had
built House Vell's standing over three generations. "Fear like that doesn't
stay contained to Cassian Ashcombe. It spreads to every house with an old
name and an older secret — including, Corwin, our own. I will not open a door
I cannot control the far side of."

"Then we do nothing?"

"We do nothing loudly. We remember everything quietly, and we let Cassian
Ashcombe govern under the weight of a debt he doesn't yet know he owes House
Vell for its silence." Meraude allowed herself, finally, something almost
like satisfaction. "That is worth considerably more to this house, in the
long run, than a scandal we couldn't fully control the shape of."

Corwin left the audience unsatisfied but obedient, uncertain whether he had
just watched his aunt show mercy or simply patience wearing mercy's clothes.
He suspected, privately, it was the latter — and that House Vell's ledger of
debts owed had simply grown one entry longer.
