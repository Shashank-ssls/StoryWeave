Chapter 11: What the Duchess Knew

Meraude Vell kept her daughter's old room exactly as it had been left, which
the household understood as grief and which was, in fact, something closer to
patience. She did not believe Sable was dead. She had never believed it,
whatever the Chancery's tax rolls recorded for convenience, and every season
she quietly paid a Bone Market informant to tell her nothing had been found —
which was, itself, a kind of finding.

"You're doing it again," Tobin Vell said, finding her at the doorway of the
empty room. Her son had his father's patience and none of his mother's
stubbornness, which made him easy to love and hard to rely on in a crisis.

"Doing what."

"Standing here. Waiting for a door that isn't going to open."

"She's alive, Tobin."

"You don't know that."

"I know that a body was never found, and a mother is never wrong about a thing
like that, whatever the law prefers to assume." Meraude closed the door,
finally, gently. "If she wanted to be found, she would let herself be found.
Until then, I will not spend House Vell's strength chasing a ghost who doesn't
want catching."

"And if she never wants catching?"

Meraude didn't answer that. She had asked herself the same question a hundred
times since the disappearance, and had never once liked any answer she'd found
for it, so she had stopped looking for one and started, instead, simply
waiting — the same patience the empty room itself had learned to keep.
