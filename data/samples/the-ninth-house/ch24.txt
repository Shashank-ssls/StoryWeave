Chapter 24: Marrowfall

They rode out from Aldenreach before dawn, Thessaly, Vesper, and Sorrel
wrapped in plain traveling cloaks, and reached Marrowfall by the time the sun
had fully cleared the eastern hills. Sorrel had expected ruins to look more
dramatic than they did — broken towers, perhaps, or blackened stone. Instead
Marrowfall was simply quiet: a manor house slowly returning to the hillside
around it, ivy where tapestries had hung, birds nesting in a hall that had
once, Thessaly said, held nine seats at its high table and only ever filled
eight after the fall.

"The Long Silence," Thessaly said, walking the empty hall with the ease of
long familiarity. "Three centuries ago the crown decided the Ninth House's
soul-craft was a danger to the realm rather than a service to it, and moved
against Marrowfall in a single night. Some of the House escaped. Most did
not. The ones who escaped learned, very quickly, never to be loud again."

In a locked cellar room, behind a door Vesper opened with a key she wore on a
cord beneath her collar, they found what they had come for: the Marrow Seal,
a heavy disc of tarnished silver, kept in a case that had clearly not been
opened in some time.

"It answers to Marrow blood," Thessaly said, and held it out to Sorrel with
no more ceremony than handing over a set of household keys. "Take it. See
what it does."

Sorrel took it. Nothing happened at all — no warmth, no light, no sense of
recognition passing between silver and skin. She looked up, half expecting
disappointment on Thessaly's face, and found something far more troubling
there instead: careful, quiet concern.
