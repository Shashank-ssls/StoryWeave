Chapter 29: An Argument in the Archive

"He has a right to know what's happening to him," Vesper said, pacing the
Undercroft's narrow floor in a way she rarely allowed herself to. "You'd tell
Sorrel. You did tell Sorrel."

"Sorrel came looking. She asked the questions herself, over months, and
earned the answers as she went." Thessaly did not look up from her ledger,
which Vesper had long since learned meant the opposite of indifference. "The
Regent of Thornmere collapsed in his study without ever once suspecting there
was a question to ask. Telling him what he is, unprepared, in the middle of
governing a realm that only just buried its last king, could break something
in him that three centuries of careful silence were built to prevent."

"Or it could save his life the next time his own soul turns on him without
warning."

"Both things can be true. That's precisely the difficulty." Thessaly set down
her pen at last. "I am not refusing forever, Vesper. I am refusing tonight,
while a Ninth House migraine and a resentful Vell cousin's suspicions are
already more fire than the Choir can safely stand near at once."

Neither of them noticed Mira Quell in the archive's far corner, meant to be
sorting old correspondence, who had gone very still somewhere around the word
*Regent* and had not moved since. She understood only pieces of what she'd
overheard — a name, a sickness, a secret too large for the room to hold
comfortably — but she understood enough to know it was not hers to repeat, and
carried it home that night the careful, frightened way a child carries
something they suspect might be dangerous even before they understand why.
