Chapter 2: The First Sitting

The Regency Council met for the first time three days after the funeral, in a hall
built for a king who no longer needed it. Cassian Ashcombe took the Regent's chair
without ceremony, as though sitting in it were simply the next item of business.
His sister, Ione Ashcombe, stood behind him rather than beside him, which Sorrel —
carrying the water-damaged ledger she had been assigned to copy — noticed and did
not understand until much later.

Duchess Meraude Vell arrived last, which was its own kind of statement, and took
the seat directly across from Cassian with the ease of someone who considered the
distance between them temporary. Lord Fennick Oswald and his sister Brenna Oswald
sat nearer the door, a smaller house content to be useful rather than central.

Ser Robart Kell, Cassian's guard-captain, stood at the Regent's shoulder the whole
sitting and said nothing, which Sorrel decided was probably his job.

The business itself was dull — grain counts, the Salt Tithe's collection schedule,
a dispute over Thornmere Succession Law and whether the Choosing could be called
before a full year's mourning had passed. Meraude argued for sooner. Cassian did
not argue at all, only listened, which somehow won the room more than arguing
would have.

Sorrel's ledger was set aside for her to finish elsewhere — water had blurred half
its pages into a private, illegible cipher only patience would unpick. She carried
it home that night thinking of grain counts, not of ciphers, and did not yet know
how wrong that would turn out to be.
