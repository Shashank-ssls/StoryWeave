Chapter 28: The Old Name, Worn Thin

Maester Ysolde Fenn had treated enough soul-sight migraines that season to
recognize a pattern when she saw one, and Cassian Ashcombe's collapse in his
own study — found by Thorne, unconscious over an untouched glass of wine — was
plainly the same affliction, advanced considerably further than anything
she'd yet seen at a masque. Conventional physic had done nothing for the
masked guests. She doubted it would do more for the Regent. So, quietly, and
at some risk to her own standing, she sent word through a Quarter apothecary
she trusted to a woman she knew only as Vesper, asking for anything at all
that might help.

Thessaly came herself, that night, brought in through a servants' passage
Thorne did not ask questions about, because some part of him — the part that
had lied about being merely tired, months ago, and never examined why —
already suspected he did not want the answer.

She examined the Regent for a long while in silence, Kaelen's Grimoire open
on the desk beside her, unread but present, as though its nearness alone
might confirm what she already believed. Then Thessaly held the grimoire
beside the Regent's sleeping face and said the old name plainly: Kaelen, worn
thin over three centuries, had woken up as Cassian Ashcombe.

Ysolde, watching from the doorway, understood perhaps a tenth of what that
sentence meant and knew immediately it was the truest thing anyone had said
in that room all night.

"Will he live," Ysolde asked, because it was the only question her training
had equipped her to ask first.

"He'll live," Thessaly said. "Whether he'll thank any of us for it, once he
understands what he's carrying, is a different question entirely — and not
one I intend to answer for him tonight."
